# 2026-10-26 — MTP on Qwen3.8-27B-FP8, 2x V100-SXM2

Status: **works; decode 2.0-2.3x over the graphed baseline.**

The checkpoint carries its own MTP layer (`mtp_num_hidden_layers=1`, with
`mtp.fc`, `mtp.layers.0.mlp.{gate,up,down}_proj` in the index), so
`--speculative-algorithm NEXTN` uses the target model's head and needs no
separate draft model. This is the engine's speculative path, not a restomod
change: nothing in `sglang_restomod` is involved beyond the FP8 dense path
already in use.

## What was run

Same stack as the CUDA graph record (fork `dg1kjd/sglang-sxm2` @ `73abfe1`
plus the lab patches), fp16 KV, TP2, graph on:

```sh
SGLANG_27B_MODEL=/homes/pding/qlab/models/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 \
SGLANG_27B_QUANT=fp8 SGLANG_27B_TP=2 SGLANG_27B_DISABLE_GRAPH=0 \
SGLANG_27B_SPEC=NEXTN SGLANG_27B_SPEC_STEPS=3 SGLANG_27B_SPEC_TOPK=1 \
SGLANG_27B_SPEC_DRAFT=4 \
  bash scripts/serve_sglang_27b_v100.sh
```

Unlike Flash-Next there is no QSA ring here, so the 4-draft-token cap does
not apply; 3 steps / topk 1 / 4 tokens is simply the EAGLE chain shape.

## Results

Streaming requests, unique prefix per request, 128 output tokens, warmup
request discarded:

| Context | Graph | MTP | Decode tok/s | Accept len | Accept rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 380 tok | on | off | 34.70 | — | — |
| 380 tok | on | on | **67.6-78.8** | 2.61-3.05 | 0.54-0.69 |
| 2.7k tok | on | off | 33.67 | — | — |
| 2.7k tok | on | on | **66.9-74.5** | 3.12-3.46 | 0.70-0.84 |
| 8.2k tok | on | on | **41.8-44.5** | 3.28-3.46 | 0.75-0.81 |

Decode falls from about 74 tok/s at 2.7k to about 43 at 8.2k while the accept
length rises to 3.3-3.5 — the draft gets easier with more context, but the
verify step gets slower, so context dominates.

Prefill, measured separately with `max_new_tokens=1` and a unique prefix per
request: **1,680-1,708 tok/s at 2k** and **1,331-1,364 tok/s at 8k** once the
process is warm. The first 8k prefill after startup is slower (893 tok/s here,
300-733 in the earlier run) because the prefill kernels are still being JIT
compiled; a benchmark that quotes prefill has to discard that request.

The "off" column is the earlier CUDA graph record at 1K and 8K context.

Accept length is higher than Flash-Next's 2.3-2.9 at the same draft depth.
A dense model is easier to draft for than a 512-expert MoE, which is the
expected direction.

The first long prefill after startup took 21.0 s (130 tok/s); the second
took 1.62 s (1691 tok/s). The first is JIT of the prefill kernel for that
shape, so a benchmark has to warm up before quoting prefill numbers.

## Long context

Started with `--context-length 131072`. The pool sized itself to **191,440
tokens** (K and V 2.92 GB each per rank, fp16), so the request is not the
limit — memory is, and it lands well past 128K.

Measured with unique prefixes, 64 output tokens:

| Context | Prefill | Decode | Accept len |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8k | 1,330 tok/s | 42-45 tok/s | 3.3-3.5 |
| 32k | **704 tok/s** | **21.6 tok/s** | 3.37 |
| 64k | **431 tok/s** | **9.3 tok/s** | 2.56 |

Decode halves from 8k to 32k and roughly halves again to 64k, i.e. it scales
with context rather than plateauing. Prefill falls 1.9x going from 8k to 32k
and another 1.6x from 32k to 64k, so cost per token grows sub-linearly but
substantially.

The practical reading: 32k is comfortable, 64k works but at 9 tok/s is not
interactive, and the KV footprint at 191k tokens (5.8 GB per rank) is what
caps it rather than the configured context length.

## KV dtype: e5m2 trades prefill for decode

Same long-context benchmark with `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e5m2`. The output is
corrupt (#51) but the timings are valid, and accept length is close enough
(3.05 vs 3.37 at 32k) that MTP is not the confound.

| Context | fp16 KV | e5m2 KV |
|---|---|---|
| 32k prefill | 704 tok/s | **1,464 tok/s** |
| 64k prefill | 431 tok/s | **1,128 tok/s** |
| 32k decode | **21.6 tok/s** | 4.9 tok/s |
| 64k decode | **9.3 tok/s** | 2.4 tok/s |

Prefill roughly doubles, decode is about four times slower. Interactive use is
decode-bound, so e5m2 is a net loss here even before the correctness problem.

One caveat that matters for interpreting it: the hand-written CUDA decode
partial did **not** load in this run — `CUDA long-context decode partial loaded`
never appears, and neither does the `disabled` line. Every run where it did load
had CUDA graph off, while this one had the graph on. So this is e5m2 with the
TileLang decode kernel, not the fast path the partial was written to be.

## What was tried and rejected: W4A16

The obvious next lever after MTP is 4-bit weights, since decode at 8k reads
about 13.5 GB of weights per rank per token against roughly 0.5 GB of KV. The
`Qwen3.8-27B-W4A16-AutoRound` checkpoint with `--quantization compressed-tensors`
runs, but is much worse:

| Checkpoint | 1k decode | 8k decode | Accept len |
|---|---|---|---|
| FP8 + MTP | 67.6-78.8 | 41.8-44.5 | 2.6-3.5 |
| W4A16 + MTP | **12.3-14.1** | **11.8-12.7** | **1.01** |

The accept rate is **0.000** — no draft is ever accepted, so MTP contributes
nothing and the whole cost is paid per token. The loader also reports
`Acceleration for non-quantized schemes is not supported by Compressed Tensors.
Falling back to UnquantizedLinearMethod`, i.e. part of the model is not on the
4-bit path at all.

This matches what 1Cat recorded in their issue #721 (about 3x slower with MTP
acceptance at 0%), so it is the checkpoint family rather than our stack. FP8
stays the right choice for this model, and the 4-bit direction is blocked by
both kernel speed and speculative decoding.

## Correctness

Greedy, four prompts, graph and MTP both on:

```
The capital of France is  -> Paris.
The capital of Germany is -> Berlin.
The capital of Italy is   -> Rome.
The capital of Spain is   -> Madrid.
```

All four correct. This also does not reproduce the one-off first-request
anomaly noted in the CUDA graph record.

## Compared with the vLLM recipe

Measured at matched context, which is worse than the 2.7k number suggested:

| At 8K context | this stack (FP8, MTP) | 1Cat (NVFP4, DFlash2 x7) |
|---|---|---|
| decode | 41.8-44.5 tok/s | ~130 tok/s |
| prefill | 1,331-1,364 tok/s | 2,373 tok/s |

So the gap at 8K is about 3x on decode and about 1.75x on prefill. Decode is
the bigger deficit, and the two stack differences that matter more than the
draft both point at it: 1Cat runs **NVFP4** weights rather than FP8, and
**fp8_e4m3 KV** rather than fp16. Halving the KV bytes read per token is a
plausible explanation for why our decode falls off faster with context — which
makes the e5m2 KV path (#51) the largest single lever here, not a side issue.

That makes the draft model the next thing to try rather than another
kernel: the fork already registers DFLASH, and `Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2` is
the checkpoint 1Cat uses.
